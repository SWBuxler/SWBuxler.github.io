Then AI arrived. So does any of that still matter?

It's the honest question, and every engineer graduating right now is being asked it — usually by someone who already thinks they know the answer.

05 · the answer

It matters more. It's what tells me which mode to use.

AI amplifies whatever judgment you bring to it. Point it at a codebase you don't understand and it will help you make a bigger mess, faster. Five years of shipping taught me where to reach for it and where not to.

hand-built

  • Machine control and field devices A wrong branch has a physical consequence and no undo.
  • Money-correct paths Positions and hedges have to be reproducible, and defensible to an auditor a year later.
  • Domain logic with no precedent Basis, carry, spreads. A model returns something confident and wrong; you have to already know the answer.

ai-driven

  • Greenfield tools Small blast radius, fast feedback, a human looking at it before it matters.
  • Breadth work Integrations, migrations, glue, test scaffolding. Lots of surface, little novelty.
  • Exploration Three prototypes in the time one used to take, to find the right shape.

Neither mode is the better one. Choosing wrong is expensive in both directions — over-automate the trading path and you ship a plausible-looking error into something that moves money; hand-write the internal dashboard and you burn a week proving you didn't need to.
