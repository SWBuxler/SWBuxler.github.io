Interactive Commodity Trading Positions Report

Visualize and intuitively examine trading contracts in tabular form.

Date Made
Summer 2025
Technologies
Python, Django, Vue.js

The problem

One form of data our customers tracked on our app was contracts - used to track agreements to buy or sell a commodity. Our goal was to display this contract data in a user-friendly table where contracts could be filtered and grouped into logical units. We also wanted to let our users interact with the table to view the details of individual contracts, but at a more manageable scale than looking through the entire contract list.

What I built

The construction of the table itself was straightforward; the real meat of the project came with the extra features associated with the table's unique design requirements. Various filters on the frontend altered which data was shown on the table without having to send a request to the backend with every filter change, a design choice made to keep filter changes fast and responsive. All the filtering, selection, and aggregation required speedy TypeScript implementation, which I created mostly by hand with the use of some limited AI tools for specific issues.

Challenges

As mentioned in the previous section, one of the main priorities was making sure that the program would respond quickly to user input. My solution to this issue was to control what data was transmitted to the frontend and how it was structured, in addition to making sure the filtering operations were as streamlined as possible to minimize the number of times they would have to iterate over the complete dataset. Another approached I took to addressing the latency issue was making these operations asynchronous, which helped to keep the UI from freezing while the filtering was taking place.

What I learned

The position report table was a heavily user-facing feature, and accordingly, I had to dedicate as much effort to making sure the table did its job in an intuitive, smooth, and user-friendly way as I did to ensuring it did its job correctly. In doing so, I got a lot of experience with evaluating the "feel" of a piece of software as well as its technical correctness, which I have found to be a valuable skill for working with frontend applications, and one I use often.
