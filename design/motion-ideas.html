What I'd actually build

The trap with a motion-heavy portfolio is that it starts reading as a demo of animations rather than a case for hiring someone. The fix is picking one signature moment and keeping everything else quiet and functional.

Recommended · "Trading Desk in motion" 01 Ticker Tape as a permanent top strip · 02 Candlestick Draw once behind the hero · 17 Video Peek and 18 Underline Draw on interaction · 19 Timeline Draw and 20 Progress Rail doing structural work. One loud idea, everything else earning its keep.
Quieter · "Instrument" 04 Contour Map at very low opacity · 10 Counting Numbers once · 15 Row Expand · 18 Underline Draw · 19 Timeline. Nothing loops forever; nothing moves unprompted.
Boldest · "Quote Board" 11 Split-Flap for the name · 01 Ticker · 03 Field Rows behind the About page. Genuinely distinctive, genuinely polarizing. Only if he's staying in ag or commodities.

Three rules whichever way you go

  1. Nothing that loops forever near text. Ambient motion belongs behind a hero or a footer, never beside a paragraph someone is trying to read.
  2. prefers-reduced-motion is not optional. Some readers get motion sick, and some reviewers check. This page honors it — every animation here stops dead if your OS asks it to.
  3. Everything must be readable at rest. If a recruiter screenshots the hero, the still frame has to work. No content that only appears after an animation finishes.

Give me a combination — one of the three above, or your own picks by number — and I'll build it into site.css on top of whichever visual direction you chose from the eight.
